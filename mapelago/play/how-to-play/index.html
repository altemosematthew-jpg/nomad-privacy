Mapelago

How to play Mapelago

Mapelago is a daily puzzle with one mystery city. You earn clues by sliding tiles on a small board, then you pin the city on a world map. A game takes about three minutes.

The four steps

  1. Slide the tiles. Swipe on a phone, or use the arrow keys or the on-screen arrows. Two tiles with the same number join into one tile worth double.
  2. Earn clues. The first time you make a new tile size, the game gives you a clue about the city.
  3. Drop a pin. Open the map whenever you like and tap where you think the city is. You get three pins.
  4. Read the miss. A pin that misses tells you how far off it was and which way to go.

The six clues

The tiles change color as they grow, from open water to land, like the depths on a sea chart.

Scoring

You start with 1,000 points. Every move costs 8 points and every missed pin costs 150. Put a pin within 50 km of the city and you've found it. Your score is the points you have left.

If all three pins miss, you keep part of your points. The closer your best pin was, the more you keep. A best pin 2,500 km away or farther keeps nothing.

So the puzzle is a trade. More moves buy more clues, and every clue costs points. A careful player spends about 350 points to earn all six.

The countries version

Mystery country plays the same way, with a country as the answer. The six clues are its hemisphere, continent, coast and borders, languages, the first letter of its capital and, last, its outline. Put a pin inside the country, or within 50 km of it, and you've found it.

Voyage, the trivia trip

Voyage is ten islands with one question on each: capitals, outlines, borders, a mark on the globe, sizes and languages. Each right answer is worth more, up to 1,000 points. After each one you can drop anchor and keep your points, or sail on. A wrong answer ends the trip and leaves you with the points from the last harbor, at islands 3 and 6. You get one spyglass a day, which takes away two wrong answers.

The quick games: Bigger city? and Bigger country?

Bigger country? is the same game with countries and land area.

Bigger city? takes about a minute. You see ten pairs of cities and tap the one where more people live. The pairs start far apart and get closer. Everyone gets the same ten pairs each day, and your result is a row of ten colored squares.

Good to know

  • Everyone in the world gets the same city and the same starting tiles, so you can compare results with friends.
  • A new city arrives at midnight, your time. Mondays are the easiest and Sundays the hardest.
  • If no tile can move, or your points reach zero, the board locks and you have to pin with the clues you have.
  • Your result is a row of colored squares with arrows. It never shows the city, so it's safe to post.
  • You can change how the game looks. Tap the half-filled circle at the top of the game and pick one of six looks. The rules and the scoring are the same in every one.
  • It's free. There's no account and nothing to install.
