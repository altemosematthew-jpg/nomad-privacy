A new US chip rule hits 5 countries | China warns of countermeasures

Chinese AI firms have been renting computing power on US chips in data centers abroad. On Monday, Washington moved to close that route. Watch to the end.

What happened:
- The US Commerce Department issued an interim rule on Monday. Shipping advanced AI chips to data centers in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil will need a US license.
- Large data centers anywhere that give China-headquartered companies remote access to that computing power also need a license. Those already doing it get 90 days.
- US-headquartered companies can be exempt if they report to the government.

Why:
- Commerce says the five countries carry a "higher-than-normal risk" of chips being diverted.
- Reuters has reported Chinese AI firms rushing to rent Nvidia-powered computing abroad before the rule.

The market:
- SMH, a fund of big chip stocks, fell 5.1% on Monday, the day the rule came out. Nvidia's leadership turmoil weighed too.

China's reply:
- Foreign ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun called the rule "typical economic coercion" and said China will take necessary measures.
- China's commerce ministry says it will take countermeasures if the US persists, and urges talks.
- The rule's own text calls it a "logical extension of existing controls."

Coming up:
- Xi hosts APEC leaders in Shenzhen on 18–19 November. Trump is expected to attend.
