The trade truce got 2 more months | Trump and Xi meet next week

The US–China trade truce was due to run out on Tuesday. Here's what stays on hold, and what could still change. Watch to the end.

What happened:
- On Monday, China extended its side of the truce by two months, to 10 January 2027.
- Its extra 24% tariff on US goods stays suspended. A 10% tariff still applies.
- Its curbs on exporting rare earths and other critical minerals stay paused, and so do special port fees on US-linked ships.
- The US paused action in its own shipping probe and extended 178 tariff exclusions.

Why markets care:
- China refined 91% of the world's rare earths in 2024 and mined 60%, according to the IEA.
- FXI, a fund of the 50 largest Chinese stocks listed in Hong Kong, is up 2.5% this month. The Hang Seng rose 0.4% on Tuesday, the day the extension began.

The catch:
- It's a pause, not a final deal. Bessent says the goal is to "de-risk, not to decouple," and that the US will "closely assess" China's follow-through on rare earths and farm purchases.
- China says it will "closely follow" US plans for new Section 301 tariffs and may adjust its countermeasures.

Coming up:
- Trump and Xi are expected to meet at the APEC summit in Shenzhen on 18–19 November.
