Oil is betting on peace | The tankers aren't

A strike hit the pipeline that carries about 4% of the world's oil, and crude still fell. Watch to the end.

What happened:
- On Monday, Saudi Arabia said its East-West pipeline was attacked in the Medina region. A limited fire was put out and flows were not affected.
- The pipeline moves about 4 million barrels a day to the Red Sea, around the Strait of Hormuz.
- Brent crude briefly slipped under $90 on Tuesday, a first since August. The Brent oil fund (BNO) is down 18% since 30 October.

Why oil fell anyway:
- Trump told CNN new strikes on Iran are "highly unlikely" if a strong deal is signed.
- Qatar is carrying proposals between Washington and Tehran.
- US-brokered Russia–Ukraine talks restarted in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday.

The catch:
- Shipping a barrel to China costs about $20 more when it loads inside the Gulf than just outside Hormuz.
- Between 11 and 16 tankers have been hit in the strait this month, depending on who is counting.
- Iran wants seven demands met before talks go further, including lifting the US blockade of its ports and lifting sanctions. Rubio calls some of them "not realistic"; Iran calls its plan "the most logical" one.
- A deal reopened Hormuz in June. It held for only a few weeks.
